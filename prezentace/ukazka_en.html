One job, start to finish — in brief

The whole process, from assigning a colour range to a print position all the way to an ink mixed to the size of the job, the mesh openness and the real size of the logo. Each button jumps to the scene that shows the step; a step without a button has no scene of its own yet.

stepwhat happensscene
1 · colour range Every print position on a product has its colour ranges assigned — whoever mixes sees only the inks allowed for that spot and its technology.
2 · the job The spec arrives by dragging the PDF in, by barcode reader, or typed by hand — product, shade, quantity and print position are pulled out automatically.
3 · technology The technology is set by the print position, not by a global setting — one product is routinely printed with several technologies.
4 · recipe A standard from a purchased range, the shop's own shade derived from an existing recipe (valid only on its product), or a work-in-progress colour from the spec. Whatever was mixed for the combination last time is offered first. —
5 · size of the logo Coverage is computed from the points where ink actually lands, not from the box around the artwork — 25.71 → 3.25 cm².
6 · mesh openness How much ink the mesh lets through: open area × fabric thickness × transfer factor, or etch depth of the cliché in pad printing. Without manufacturer data, indicative only.
7 · the batch Area × quantity × g/m², plus losses in per cent — and the minimum batch, the floor below which the app will not go.
8 · leftovers Stock first: a cup with the identical composition is simply poured over; for a different shade the app works out what to add.
9 · mixing A mixing ticket with cumulative weighing, and the assistant at the scale walks through component by component; an overpour is levelled out by growing the whole batch, keeping the shade.
10 · the label The cup gets a barcode label; after the job the leftover goes back into the register and the loop closes.

The figures come from the A3 P26-31-01 INK and DAC improvements, data source INK repairs.xlsx (problemove_barvy.xlsm), measured 2 Apr — 10 Aug 2026. Nothing here is an estimate except a single value, which the A3 itself marks as to be confirmed — and that one sits on the slider below.

The app uses the scale and the barcode reader the shop already owns and runs from a single file — nothing has to be bought for it.

What the saving does not include yet

Material saving is carried in the A3 as “not quantified yet”. One single job has been measured: 138823, where consumption fell from 3.1 to 0.4 g per piece (area 25.71 → 3.25 cm², eight times less). It cannot be scaled to the whole shop — there are no shop-wide saving percentages and one job is not enough to derive them.

On top of that comes the material binned today as an unlabelled leftover. The leftover register puts it back into circulation and the reports have been adding it up since 18 Aug 2026 — how much of it there is per year will be known once the register has run for a few months.
